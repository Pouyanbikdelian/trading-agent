## POST

I spent weeks trying to make my trend-following system faster. Shorter lookback windows, tighter rebalance cycles, quicker rotations out of positions.

All the backtests got worse.

The real surprise wasn't that it failed—it's *why*. The faster version was exiting winners too early. You catch the first move, book the gain, then rotate out before the compounding leg. Meanwhile, slower configs just held and let the winners extend. Over years, that friction difference compounds.

It's a lesson I should've learned from first principles, but I had to watch it fail in data. Trend-following doesn't win because you react fast. It wins because you hold the right thing long enough for the trend to actually work.

That's what live testing is for. It forces you to stop optimizing and start observing.

Paper trading through Interactive Brokers. Still learning what actually matters versus what feels smart.
