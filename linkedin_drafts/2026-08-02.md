## POST

For months I assumed I was diversified because I held different things.

Then I actually measured it. Two groups of holdings I'd treated as unrelated turned out to move almost exactly opposite each other once you looked at them relative to the broad market. One up, the other down, week after week. I got briefly excited. That shape looks like a free lunch.

It wasn't. The moment I accounted for what it costs to keep flipping between them, the pattern that was obvious on a chart stopped being tradeable. It was information, not an edge.

So the lesson I keep relearning: diversification isn't owning many things. It's owning things that don't share a driver. Ten positions all responding to the same rate story is one bet wearing ten costumes.

I use that relationship as context now, a read on what the market is nervous about, rather than as a signal.

Still paper trading through Interactive Brokers. Still finding that most patterns are worth knowing and not worth trading.

How do you tell a real edge from an interesting chart?
